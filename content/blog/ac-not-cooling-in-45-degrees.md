Every May, the same question comes up across Ahmedabad: the AC is running, the fan is blowing, but the room will not drop below 29°C. If your **AC is not cooling in 45 degree heat**, it is usually not broken. It is working at its limit, and small problems that go unnoticed in March show up badly at peak afternoon temperatures. Here is what is going on and what you can do about it.

## Does an AC work in 45 degree Celsius?

Yes. Air conditioners sold in India are designed for hot climates. Energy-star ratings are measured at 35°C outdoors, and most current split ACs are built to keep running well above that, with many models rated to work at 50°C or more. Check your AC's manual for its **maximum operating ambient temperature**.

The catch is that **cooling capacity drops as outdoor temperature rises**. An AC is a heat pump: it moves heat from your room to the outdoor air. The hotter the outside air, the harder it is to push heat into it. At 45°C, even a healthy 1.5 ton AC delivers noticeably less cooling than its label suggests, so anything that makes its job harder tips it over the edge.

## 7 reasons your AC is not cooling in extreme heat

### 1. A dirty outdoor condenser

This is the most common cause in Ahmedabad. The condenser coil is where heat leaves your home. When its fins are coated in dust, pollen and pigeon feathers, it cannot release heat, pressure rises and cooling falls. Many owners clean their filters but never the outdoor unit.

### 2. The outdoor unit sits in direct afternoon sun

An outdoor unit baking on a west-facing wall or an open terrace can be pulling in air hotter than 50°C. Shade helps, but never box the unit in. It needs at least a foot or two of open space around it for airflow.

### 3. Clogged filters and indoor coil

Blocked filters reduce airflow, so the room cools slowly and the indoor coil can even ice up. In dusty parts of the city, filters may need a wash every two weeks in summer.

### 4. Low refrigerant (gas)

If the indoor unit blows only mildly cool air, or you see ice on the copper pipes, a leak may have dropped the gas level. Refrigerant is not consumed, so a refill should always come with a leak check. See [signs your AC needs a gas refill](/blog/signs-your-ac-needs-a-gas-refill).

### 5. Wrong AC size for the room

A top-floor bedroom with a west wall in Ahmedabad gains far more heat than a ground-floor room of the same size. A 1 ton AC that copes in March can fall well short in May. Top-floor rooms, rooms with large windows and kitchens next door often need 1.5 or 2 tons.

### 6. Low voltage at peak hours

During heatwaves, evening demand is high and voltage can dip. Low voltage strains the compressor; older non-inverter ACs especially may shut off or cool weakly. A good stabiliser (or an inverter AC with a wide voltage range) helps.

### 7. Weak capacitor or failing fan motor

A capacitor that is starting to fail may still start the compressor in the morning but give up in the afternoon heat. If the outdoor fan is spinning slowly, or the compressor keeps clicking on and off, this is a likely cause.

## AC tripping again and again in summer?

When the condenser cannot get rid of heat, refrigerant pressure climbs and the AC's protection may cut the compressor. Other common triggers are overloaded wiring, a weak capacitor or low voltage. If the **MCB trips repeatedly**, stop resetting it and get the AC inspected; repeated tripping can damage the compressor or wiring.

## Quick fixes you can try today

1. **Clean the filters.** Slide them out, wash them in plain water and let them dry fully.
2. **Set 24–26°C, not 18°C.** The AC cools at the same rate either way; a lower setting just keeps the compressor running longer.
3. **Close curtains on sun-facing windows** from late morning, and keep doors shut.
4. **Clear space around the outdoor unit** and remove anything blocking airflow.
5. **Run a ceiling fan on low** with the AC to spread cool air faster.
6. **Pre-cool before the afternoon peak.** Start the AC earlier rather than switching it on at 3 pm when the room is already hot.
7. **Avoid heat sources** like cooking or ironing in the room during peak hours.

**Do not** spray water on a running outdoor unit or its electrical parts, and do not cover it while it is working.

## When to call a technician

Call for service if:

- The AC blows warm or barely cool air even with clean filters
- You see ice on the pipes or indoor coil
- The MCB trips more than once
- You hear hissing, grinding or loud clicking
- The outdoor fan is not running while the compressor hums

A proper check includes gas pressure, outdoor and indoor coil condition, capacitor and fan health, and supply voltage. A jet cleaning of both units often restores most of the lost cooling.
