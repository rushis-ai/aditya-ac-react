An AC doesn't "use up" refrigerant the way a car uses fuel. The gas circulates in a sealed loop, so if it's running low, something is leaking. Here's how to tell, and what to do about it.

## 1. The AC blows air, but it isn't cold

The most common sign. The fan runs normally but the air feels only slightly cool, even on the lowest setting.

## 2. The room takes far longer to cool

If a room that used to cool in 10 minutes now takes 40, low gas is a likely cause (along with dirty filters and coils, so rule those out first).

## 3. Ice on the copper pipes or indoor coil

Low pressure makes the coil too cold, and moisture freezes on it. It looks odd, but it's a classic low-refrigerant symptom.

## 4. A hissing or bubbling sound

A small hiss near the pipes or outdoor unit can mean gas is escaping.

## 5. Higher electricity bills

The compressor runs longer to reach the set temperature, which shows up on your bill.

## 6. Water dripping from the indoor unit

Ice on the coil melts when the AC switches off and can overflow the drain tray.

## 7. The outdoor unit runs but the fan feels warm

The outdoor fan should blow out hot air when cooling is working. Weak or lukewarm airflow there can point to low gas.

## When it's *not* the gas

- **Dirty filters or coils:** a deep clean often restores cooling.
- **Wrong mode:** check it's on Cool, not Fan or Dry.
- **Undersized AC:** a 1-ton unit in a large, sunny room will struggle in May whatever its gas level.

## What a proper refill involves

1. Pressure check to confirm the gas really is low.
2. Leak test and repair; refilling without fixing the leak just wastes money.
3. Vacuuming the lines where needed.
4. Charging with the correct refrigerant (R32, R410A or R22) to the maker's pressure.
5. Testing the outlet temperature and current draw.

Not sure what's wrong with your AC? A technician can check pressure, airflow and coils in one visit and tell you whether you need gas at all.
